    Skill 1: Recognize what your emotion is asking for

    Each emotion on the wheel is asking for something — it wants something from us. When you can name the feeling, you can start to figure out what it needs.

    If you can't give it what it wants

    Sometimes we can't give an emotion what it's asking for. Then ask: what's left? Is there a secondary emotion underneath that I can tend to as well?

    First: tired, hungry, thirsty, triggered?

    These basic needs have to be met first, if possible. It's hard to read your emotions clearly when your body is running on empty.

    Complete the whole thought

    Take it all the way through — from noticing, to naming, to doing:

    I'm feeling this, I know it's connected to this, my body needs this, and this is what I'm going to do about it.

    Use more than one sense

    Give yourself multiple pathways of understanding. Write it down. Then say it out loud — and if you can, say it out loud into a mirror.

    Daily practice

    1. Pick a reflective time of day — a transition time works well (like after work, or before bed).
    2. Without looking at the wheel, write down some words for how you felt during the day.
    3. Then consult the wheel. Do the words match your experience? Is there a more accurate word?
    4. Complete the whole thought: what I'm feeling, and what I'm going to do about it.

    The Daily check-in walks you through these steps.

    Why build a bigger vocabulary?

    Many of us were treated as kids as if our emotional range was small — happy or sad, good or bad. So we often reach adulthood with a limited vocabulary for our own feelings. Part of this practice is building a more accurate vocabulary to describe what we actually experience.

    The Journal tab keeps track of the words you've used, so you can watch your vocabulary grow.

    A life tool, not a crisis tool

    This is something to practice every day, in ordinary moments, so it's there when you need it. It isn't meant for a crisis.

    If you're in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please reach out for support right away. In the US, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). Elsewhere, contact your local emergency number.
